# Crit 7 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

There were two, and both were about pushing back. The first was cutting the
scope. ANUHub course selection has a lot wrong with it, but the part that's
genuinely painful is adding courses one by one: add, search, confirm, and
again for the next course. Once I told the agent to focus only on that, the
work had a clear shape.

The second was pushing back on the four-course rule. The first version let you
enrol in as many courses as you liked, and every test passed, but that isn't
how ANU works: you take four courses a semester, and for international
students it's exactly four. The agent didn't know that until I told it.

Removing prerequisites was part of the same decision. Checking them would have
been adding an extra feature, not slicing, and the point was to fix the
course selection flow, not to rebuild everything around it.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It showed me that I have to tell the agent the working rules, especially for
something like an ANU system. Some of those rules aren't public, and some are
specific to the campus itself, so I'm the only one who knows what they should
be. My job is to give the agent that information, to push back when what it
builds doesn't match how things really work, and to write the rules into the
harness so it keeps behaving like an ANU system. I want to be the developer
who knows the domain well enough to do that, not one who accepts whatever
passes the tests.
